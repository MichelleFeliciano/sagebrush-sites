Concept Project · Case Study

Still Water Spa

A calm, editorial-toned day spa site built so the layout itself feels unhurried — not just the copy.

View live site → Back to portfolio

Problem

Day spas sell calm and unhurried attention — but a lot of spa websites undercut exactly that positioning with generic, cluttered templates that feel just as rushed as the experience they're trying to sell against. When the whole pitch is "an hour where nothing is asked of you," a busy, ad-heavy, slow-loading site quietly contradicts the brand before a client ever walks in.

Approach

The design goal here was restraint, not more features — and restraint meant rethinking the layout itself, not just muting the palette. There's no dense pricing grid: treatments live inside alternating text-and-photo story sections (facials, then massage, each with its own short plain-text price list), the way a spa's own site copy would walk you through a service rather than dump a menu on you. A three-card "path" section at the top helps a first-time visitor jump straight to what they came for. The photo tour of the space — treatment rooms, the quiet lounge — is woven directly into those story sections and a single full-bleed pull-quote panorama, instead of living in a separate clickable gallery grid. Reviews rotate one at a time through a single auto-advancing quote instead of a testimonial grid, so only one thought is on screen at a time — quieter, on purpose.

Tech Stack

Same offline-first foundation as the rest of the portfolio: plain HTML5, CSS3, vanilla JavaScript, single self-contained file, zero external dependencies, no build step. The only new interactive piece is the auto-advancing quote carousel — a small setInterval loop with clickable dots, restarting its timer on manual navigation so it never fights the visitor.

Result

No real client, no fabricated numbers.

Verified directly in-browser: zero external network requests, zero console errors, working quote carousel and mobile nav at phone widths. Across the four spec projects, the honest result to point to isn't a business metric — it's that the same offline-first, dependency-free foundation supports four structurally different builds (a ledger menu and filmstrip gallery here, a masonry gallery and icon menu there) instead of one template reskinned four times.
